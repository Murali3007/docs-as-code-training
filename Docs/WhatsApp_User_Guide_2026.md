# 1. Introduction
This guide walks you through WhatsApp, the mobile messaging application used by over two billion people worldwide. WhatsApp lets you send text messages, make voice and video calls, share photos and documents, and stay connected with family, friends, and colleagues. All of this works over your internet connection (Wi-Fi or mobile data), so there are no per-message charges from WhatsApp itself.

The guide is written for adults who may be new to WhatsApp or want a clearer understanding of its features. Each section includes numbered steps you can follow on your phone. The instructions apply to both Android phones and iPhones, with differences noted where they exist.

## 1.1 Purpose of this guide
This document gives you the knowledge to install, set up, and use WhatsApp on your mobile phone. It covers everyday tasks (sending messages, making calls, sharing photos) and goes into privacy settings, group management, and newer features like Channels and Communities. By the end, you should feel comfortable using WhatsApp for both personal and professional communication.

## 1.2 What you need before you start
To use WhatsApp, you need the following:

1. A smartphone running Android 5.0 or later,
or an iPhone running iOS 16 or later.

2. An active mobile phone number that can
receive SMS or phone calls (for verification).

3. An internet connection: Wi-Fi or a mobile
data plan.

4. Enough storage on your phone: at least 200
MB of free space.

**Note:** WhatsApp uses your phone number as your account ID. You cannot create an account without a working phone number.

## 1.3 Document conventions
Throughout this guide, the following conventions are used:

1. Numbered steps indicate actions you perform
in sequence.

2. Screen element names (buttons, tabs, menu
items) appear in bold when first referenced in a procedure.

3. Tip boxes contain time-saving suggestions.

4. Note boxes contain important supplementary
information.

5. Warning boxes contain information about
actions that could affect your account security or data.

# 2. Installing WhatsApp
## 2.1 Download from your app store
WhatsApp is a free download. Follow these steps to install it on your phone:

### On an Android phone
1. Open the Google Play Store app on your
phone.

2. Tap the search bar at the top of the screen
and type "WhatsApp".

3. Select "WhatsApp Messenger" from
the search results. The developer name should read "WhatsApp LLC".

4. Tap "Install". The app will
download and install automatically.

5. Once the installation is complete, tap
"Open" to launch WhatsApp.

### On an iPhone
1. Open the App Store on your iPhone.

2. Tap the search icon (magnifying glass) at
the bottom of the screen and type "WhatsApp".

3. Find "WhatsApp Messenger" by
WhatsApp LLC in the results.

4. Tap the download icon (cloud with arrow, or
"Get"). You may need to confirm with your Apple ID password or Face ID.

5. After installation, tap "Open" or
find the WhatsApp icon on your home screen.

**Tip:** If you have used WhatsApp before and have a backup, you will be asked to restore your chat history during setup. Tap "Restore" to bring back your old messages.

# 3. Setting up your account
## 3.1 Verify your phone number
When you open WhatsApp for the first time, it asks you to verify your phone number. This step confirms that the number belongs to you.

1. Select your country from the dropdown list.
WhatsApp fills in the country code automatically.

2. Enter your phone number without the leading
zero. For example, if your number is 09876543210, type 9876543210.

3. Tap "Next" or "Done".

4. WhatsApp sends a six-digit verification
code to your number via SMS. In most cases, the app detects the code automatically. If it does not, type the code into the field on screen.

5. If the SMS does not arrive within two
minutes, tap "Resend SMS" or "Call Me" to receive the code through an automated phone call.

**Warning:** Never share your verification code with anyone. WhatsApp will never ask you for this code outside the app. If someone requests it, they may be trying to take over your account.

## 3.2 Create your profile
After verification, WhatsApp asks you to create a profile. This is what other people see when they communicate with you.

1. Enter your name. Use the name you want
contacts to see (for example, your first name, or first and last name).

2. Tap the camera icon to add a profile photo.
You can take a new photo or choose one from your gallery.

3. Tap "Next" or the arrow to
proceed.

**Tip:** You can change your name and photo later in Settings > Profile. Your profile photo is visible to all your WhatsApp contacts by default, but you can restrict this in your privacy settings (covered in Section 10).

# 4. The WhatsApp interface
Once you are logged in, you will see the main WhatsApp screen. The app is organized into tabs at the bottom of the screen (iPhone) or at the top (some Android phones). Here is what each tab does:

## 4.1 Chats tab
The Chats tab is your starting point. It shows a list of all your conversations, with the most recent message at the top. Each conversation shows the contact name, a preview of the last message, and the time it was sent. Unread messages are marked with a green badge showing the count of new messages. You will also see Status updates from your contacts displayed as circular profile pictures at the top of this tab.

## 4.2 Calls tab
The Calls tab shows your call history: voice calls, video calls, and missed calls. Each entry shows the contact name, the type of call (incoming, outgoing, or missed), and when it happened. You can start a new call directly from this tab by tapping the phone icon.

## 4.3 Communities tab
The Communities tab lets you see any Communities you belong to. A Community is a collection of related groups managed by an administrator. For example, a neighborhood association might create a Community that includes groups for announcements, events, and maintenance.

## 4.4 Updates tab
The Updates tab (called "Status" on some versions) shows temporary posts from your contacts, similar to stories on other platforms. Each Status disappears after 24 hours. You will also find Channels here, which are one-way broadcast feeds from organizations, public figures, or topics you follow.

## 4.5 Settings
On iPhone, the Settings tab appears at the bottom right. On Android, tap the three-dot menu at the top right corner of the screen, then select "Settings". From here, you can manage your profile, privacy, notifications, storage, and linked devices.

# 5. Sending messages
## 5.1 Send a text message
1. Open WhatsApp and go to the Chats tab.

2. Tap the new chat icon (a pencil inside a
square on iPhone, or a message bubble on Android) at the bottom right or top right of the screen.

3. Select the contact you want to message. If
the contact uses WhatsApp, their name appears in the list.

4. Tap on the contact name to open the
conversation.

5. Tap the text input field at the bottom of
the screen (it reads "Type a message").

6. Type your message using the on-screen
keyboard.

7. Tap the green send button (arrow icon) to
send your message.

**Tip:** A single grey tick means the message left your phone. Two grey ticks mean it reached the other person's phone. Two blue ticks mean they read it.

## 5.2 Send a voice message
If you prefer to speak rather than type, you can send a voice message:

1. Open the conversation with the person you
want to message.

2. Press and hold the microphone icon to the
right of the text input field.

3. Speak your message clearly while holding
the icon.

4. Release your finger to send the voice
message.

5. To cancel a recording before sending, slide
your finger to the left while holding the microphone icon.

**Tip:** For longer voice messages, slide the microphone icon upward to lock the recording. This lets you record without holding the button the entire time. Tap the send button when you are done.

## 5.3 Reply to a specific message
When a conversation has many messages and you want to respond to a particular one:

1. Press and hold the message you want to
reply to, or swipe right on it.

2. Tap "Reply" from the menu that
appears.

3. The original message is shown above your
text input field as a reference.

4. Type your reply and tap send.

## 5.4 Forward a message
1. Press and hold the message you want to
forward.

2. Tap the forward icon (arrow pointing right)
at the top of the screen.

3. Select one or more contacts or groups you
want to send the message to.

4. Tap the send button to forward.

**Note:** Forwarded messages are labeled "Forwarded" so the recipient knows the message was not originally written by you. Messages forwarded many times carry a double-arrow icon.

## 5.5 Delete a sent message
If you sent a message by mistake, you can delete it:

1. Press and hold the message you want to
delete.

2. Tap the delete icon (trash can).

3. Choose "Delete for Everyone" to
remove it from both your phone and the recipient's phone. Choose "Delete for Me" to remove it only from your phone.

**Note:** "Delete for Everyone" works only within a limited time after sending (approximately one hour). After that, you can only delete the message from your own phone.

# 6. Sharing photos, videos, and documents
## 6.1 Send a photo or video
1. Open the conversation where you want to
share the photo or video.

2. Tap the attachment icon: a paperclip icon
on Android, or the "+" icon on iPhone, located next to the text input field.

3. Select "Photos & Videos" (or
"Gallery" on some Android phones).

4. Browse your gallery and tap the photo or
video you want to send. You can select multiple files at once.

5. Optionally, add a caption in the text field
at the bottom of the screen.

6. Tap the green send button.

**Tip:** You can also take a new photo or video by tapping the camera icon next to the text input field. This opens the camera directly within WhatsApp.

## 6.2 Send a document
WhatsApp lets you share documents such as PDFs, spreadsheets, and Word files:

1. Open the conversation.

2. Tap the attachment icon (paperclip or
"+").

3. Select "Document".

4. Browse your phone's file storage and select
the document you want to send.

5. Tap "Send".

**Note:** The maximum file size for a single document is 2 GB. Large files may take longer to upload depending on your internet speed.

## 6.3 Share your location
1. Open the conversation.

2. Tap the attachment icon.

3. Select "Location".

4. Allow WhatsApp to access your phone's
location if prompted.

5. Choose "Send your current
location" to send a one-time pin of where you are, or "Share live location" to let the other person see your position in real-time for 15 minutes, 1 hour, or 8 hours.

**Warning:** Live location sharing continuously broadcasts your position to the other person. Use this feature only with people you trust. You can stop sharing at any time by opening the chat and tapping "Stop sharing".

# 7. Making voice and video calls
WhatsApp calls use your internet connection instead of your phone's cellular minutes. Both voice and video calls are end-to-end encrypted, which means only you and the person you are calling can hear or see the conversation.

## 7.1 Make a voice call
1. Open WhatsApp and go to the Chats tab.

2. Open the conversation with the person you
want to call.

3. Tap the phone icon at the top right corner
of the screen.

4. The call connects. You will see a timer
showing the call duration.

5. Tap the red hang-up button to end the call.

**Tip:** You can also start a call from the Calls tab. Tap the phone icon with a "+" sign, then select a contact.

## 7.2 Make a video call
1. Open the conversation with the person you
want to video-call.

2. Tap the video camera icon at the top right
corner of the screen.

3. The video call connects, and you will see
the other person on your screen with your own video in a small window.

4. During the call, you can tap the camera
flip icon to switch between front and back cameras, tap the microphone icon to mute yourself, or tap the video icon to turn your camera off.

5. Tap the red hang-up button to end the call.

## 7.3 Make a group call
You can call up to 32 people at the same time on a voice or video call:

1. Open a group chat or an individual
conversation.

2. Tap the phone icon (voice) or video icon
(video) at the top right.

3. If you are in an individual chat, tap the
"Add participant" icon after the call starts to bring in more people.

4. Select the contacts you want to add and
confirm.

**Note:** Group video calls use more data than voice calls. If your internet connection is slow, participants may experience lag or lower video quality. Consider switching to a Wi-Fi connection for group calls.

# 8. Group chats
Groups are shared conversations where multiple people can read and send messages. They are useful for coordinating with family, friends, work teams, or interest-based communities. A WhatsApp group can have up to 1,024 members.

## 8.1 Create a group
1. Open WhatsApp and go to the Chats tab.

2. Tap the new chat icon, then select
"New Group".

3. Select the contacts you want to add to the
group. You need at least one other person.

4. Tap the green arrow to proceed.

5. Enter a group name (up to 100 characters).

6. Optionally, add a group icon by tapping the
camera icon.

7. Tap "Create" to finish.

## 8.2 Manage group settings
As a group admin, you can control several settings:

1. Open the group chat and tap the group name
at the top of the screen.

2. Scroll down to "Group settings".

3. Under "Edit group info", choose
whether all members or only admins can change the group name, icon, and description.

4. Under "Send messages", choose
whether all members or only admins can send messages to the group.

5. Under "Approve new participants",
choose whether new members need admin approval to join.

**Tip:** If your group is for announcements only, set "Send messages" to "Only admins". Members can still read messages but cannot reply in the group.

## 8.3 Add or remove members
To add a member:

1. Open the group chat and tap the group name.

2. Tap "Add participants".

3. Select the contact and tap the green check
mark.

To remove a member:

1. Open the group chat and tap the group name.

2. Find the member you want to remove in the
participant list.

3. Tap on their name and select "Remove
[name]".

## 8.4 Leave a group
1. Open the group chat.

2. Tap the group name at the top.

3. Scroll to the bottom and tap "Exit
group".

4. Confirm by tapping "Exit".

**Note:** After you leave a group, you will no longer receive messages from it. The group will still appear in your chat list unless you delete it.

# 9. Status updates
Status lets you share photos, videos, or text posts that disappear after 24 hours. Your contacts can view your Status, and you can see theirs. Status updates appear at the top of the Chats tab and in the Updates tab.

## 9.1 Post a Status update
1. Go to the Updates tab (or tap the Status
area at the top of the Chats tab).

2. Tap the pencil icon to create a text
Status, or tap the camera icon to share a photo or video.

3. For a text Status: type your message,
choose a background color, and tap the send button.

4. For a photo or video Status: take a new
photo/video or select one from your gallery. Add a caption if you want. Tap the send button.

## 9.2 View a contact's Status
1. Go to the Updates tab.

2. Tap on a contact's profile picture to view
their Status.

3. Tap the screen to move to the next Status
update, or swipe right to skip to the next contact's Status.

## 9.3 Control who sees your Status
1. Open Settings > Privacy > Status.

2. Choose one of three options: "My
contacts" (everyone in your address book), "My contacts except..." (exclude specific people), or "Only share with..." (allow only specific people).

3. Tap "Done" to save.

# 10. Privacy and security settings
WhatsApp provides several settings that let you control who can see your information and how your account is protected. All WhatsApp messages and calls are end-to-end encrypted by default, which means that only you and the person you are communicating with can read or listen to them. WhatsApp itself cannot access the content.

## 10.1 Control your visibility
You can decide who sees your personal information. Go to Settings > Privacy and adjust the following:

1. "Last seen & online": Choose
who can see when you were last active on WhatsApp. Options are "Everyone", "My contacts", "My contacts except...", or "Nobody".

2. "Profile photo": Choose who can
see your profile picture. Same options as above.

3. "About": Choose who can see your
About text.

4. "Read receipts": Turn this off to
hide the blue ticks that show when you have read a message. Note that if you turn this off, you will also no longer see read receipts from others.

## 10.2 Block a contact
If someone is sending you unwanted messages, you can block them:

1. Open the conversation with the person you
want to block.

2. Tap the contact name at the top of the
screen to open their profile.

3. Scroll down and tap "Block
[name]".

4. Confirm by tapping "Block".

A blocked contact cannot send you messages, call you, or see your Status updates. They will not be notified that you have blocked them.

## 10.3 Enable two-step verification
Two-step verification adds a six-digit PIN to your account. If anyone tries to register your phone number on WhatsApp again (on another device, for example), they will need this PIN.

1. Open Settings > Account > Two-step
verification.

2. Tap "Enable" or "Turn
on".

3. Enter a six-digit PIN that you will
remember.

4. Confirm the PIN by entering it again.

5. Optionally, add an email address. This lets
you reset your PIN if you forget it.

**Warning:** If you forget your PIN and did not add an email address, you will have to wait seven days before you can reset it. During that time, you will not be able to verify your account on a new phone.

## 10.4 Chat lock
Chat lock lets you move specific conversations behind a password, fingerprint, or face recognition:

1. Open the conversation you want to lock.

2. Tap the contact or group name at the top.

3. Tap "Chat lock".

4. Turn on "Lock this chat with
fingerprint" (or Face ID on iPhone).

Locked chats are hidden from your main chat list. To access them, pull down on the chat list and authenticate with your fingerprint or face recognition.

## 10.5 Advanced chat privacy
For sensitive conversations, WhatsApp offers an advanced chat privacy option that prevents the other person from exporting the chat, forwarding your messages, or using AI features on the conversation:

1. Open the conversation.

2. Tap the contact or group name at the top.

3. Tap "Advanced chat privacy".

4. Turn on the toggle.

# 11. WhatsApp Channels
Channels are one-way broadcast feeds. Organizations, businesses, sports teams, news outlets, and public figures use Channels to send updates to followers. You can follow a Channel to receive its posts, but you cannot reply to them the way you reply in a regular chat.

## 11.1 Find and follow a Channel
1. Go to the Updates tab.

2. Tap "Find Channels" or the
"+" icon in the Channels section.

3. Browse the directory or search for a
specific Channel by name.

4. Tap on a Channel to preview its content.

5. Tap "Follow" to subscribe.

## 11.2 Unfollow a Channel
1. Open the Channel.

2. Tap the Channel name at the top.

3. Tap "Unfollow".

# 12. Communities
A Community brings together related groups under one umbrella. For example, a school might set up a Community with separate groups for each class, for parents, and for announcements. Community admins can broadcast messages to all groups at once through the Announcement group.

## 12.1 Join a Community
Communities are invite-based. A Community admin can add you directly or share an invite link with you. Tap the link to join.

## 12.2 Browse groups within a Community
1. Open the Communities tab.

2. Tap on the Community name.

3. You will see a list of groups inside the
Community. Tap "Join" next to any group you want to be part of.

**Note:** A Community can hold up to 100 groups and up to 2,000 members. You are not automatically added to every group inside a Community. You choose which ones to join.

# 13. WhatsApp Web and linked devices
WhatsApp Web lets you use WhatsApp on your computer's web browser while your phone stays connected. This is convenient when you are working at a desk and want to type with a full keyboard.

## 13.1 Link your phone to a computer
1. Open a web browser on your computer and go
to web.whatsapp.com.

2. On your phone, open WhatsApp and go to
Settings > Linked devices.

3. Tap "Link a device".

4. Point your phone's camera at the QR code
displayed on your computer screen.

5. Once the code is scanned, your WhatsApp
chats will appear on the computer browser within a few seconds.

**Tip:** You can link up to four devices to your WhatsApp account at the same time. Linked devices work independently, so your phone does not need to stay online for the computer session to work.

## 13.2 Unlink a device
1. On your phone, go to Settings > Linked
devices.

2. Tap on the device you want to remove.

3. Tap "Log out".

# 14. Managing storage and data
Over time, WhatsApp accumulates photos, videos, and documents that take up space on your phone. You can review and clear this stored media.

## 14.1 Check storage usage
1. Open Settings > Storage and data >
Manage storage.

2. WhatsApp shows a summary of how much space
it is using on your phone.

3. You will see a list of conversations sorted
by the amount of storage each one uses.

4. Tap a conversation to see the individual
files. Select the files you want to delete and tap the trash icon.

## 14.2 Control automatic downloads
By default, WhatsApp downloads incoming photos automatically. To reduce data usage:

1. Open Settings > Storage and data.

2. Under "Media auto-download",
configure separate settings for "When using mobile data", "When connected on Wi-Fi", and "When roaming".

3. For each network type, choose which media
types (photos, audio, videos, documents) should download automatically.

**Tip:** If you are on a limited data plan, turn off auto-download for videos and documents when using mobile data. You can always tap on a received file to download it manually.

# 15. Backing up your chats
A backup saves your messages and media to your cloud storage account (Google Drive on Android, iCloud on iPhone). If you lose your phone or switch to a new one, you can restore your conversations from the backup.

## 15.1 Set up automatic backups
### On Android
1. Open Settings > Chats > Chat backup.

2. Tap "Back up to Google Drive".

3. Choose a backup frequency: Daily, Weekly,
or Monthly.

4. Select the Google account you want to use
for the backup. If you have not linked a Google account, tap "Add account" and sign in.

5. Under "Back up over", choose
whether to back up over Wi-Fi only or Wi-Fi and cellular data.

### On iPhone
1. Open Settings > Chats > Chat Backup.

2. Tap "Auto Backup" and choose
Daily, Weekly, or Monthly.

3. Your backup is saved to iCloud. Make sure
you are signed in to iCloud on your iPhone and have enough iCloud storage.

## 15.2 Turn on encrypted backups
For additional security, you can encrypt your cloud backup:

1. Open Settings > Chats > Chat Backup
\> End-to-end encrypted backup.

2. Tap "Turn on".

3. Create a password or use a 64-digit
encryption key.

4. Tap "Create" to start the
encrypted backup.

**Warning:** If you forget your backup password and do not have the encryption key, you will not be able to restore that backup. WhatsApp cannot reset this password for you. Write it down and keep it in a safe place.

# 16. New features in 2026
WhatsApp regularly adds new features. Here are the notable additions available as of 2026:

## 16.1 Usernames
You can now create a unique username for your WhatsApp account. This means you can share your username with someone instead of giving them your phone number. To set one up, go to Settings > Profile \> Username. Choose a username that has not already been taken. Once set, people can find and message you using your username.

## 16.2 AI writing assistance
WhatsApp includes a feature that suggests different ways to phrase your message. When you type a message, you may see an option to rewrite it in a different tone (professional, friendly, or supportive). This can help when you are writing a work-related message and want to adjust the tone before sending.

## 16.3 AI photo editing
Before sending a photo, you can use built-in editing tools to remove objects from the image, change the background, or apply artistic styles. These tools appear in the photo editor when you select a photo to send.

## 16.4 Voice message transcripts
If you receive a voice message and cannot listen to it at the moment, WhatsApp can convert it to text. To enable this, go to Settings > Chats > Voice message transcripts. The transcription happens on your phone, not on a remote server, so your voice messages remain private.

## 16.5 WhatsApp Web calling
You can now make and receive voice and video calls directly from WhatsApp Web in your computer's browser. You do not need to install the desktop application. The calls support screen sharing and reactions, and they are end-to-end encrypted.

## 16.6 Cross-platform chat transfer
If you are switching from an iPhone to an Android phone (or the other way around), WhatsApp now lets you transfer your chat history between platforms. During the setup of your new phone, scan the QR code displayed on your old phone to move your messages, photos, and videos.

# 17. Troubleshooting common issues
## 17.1 Messages are not sending
If your messages show a single grey tick and do not deliver:

1. Check that your phone is connected to the
internet. Open a web browser and try loading a website.

2. If you are using mobile data, make sure
WhatsApp has permission to use data. Go to your phone's Settings > Apps > WhatsApp > Data usage.

3. Restart your phone and try again.

4. If the problem continues, try switching
between Wi-Fi and mobile data.

## 17.2 Calls are dropping or have poor quality
1. Move to an area with a stronger Wi-Fi
signal or better mobile data coverage.

2. Close other apps that may be using your
internet connection.

3. On a video call, turn off your camera to
reduce data usage and see if the audio improves.

4. If you are on a Wi-Fi network, try moving
closer to the router.

## 17.3 WhatsApp is using too much storage
1. Go to Settings > Storage and data >
Manage storage.

2. Review and delete large files you no longer
need.

3. Turn off auto-download for videos and
documents (see Section 14.2).

4. Clear individual chat media by opening a
chat, tapping the contact name, selecting "Media, links, and docs", and deleting files from there.

## 17.4 You forgot your two-step verification PIN
1. If you set up an email address during
two-step verification setup, tap "Forgot PIN" and check your email for a reset link.

2. If you did not add an email, you will need
to wait seven days. After seven days, you can verify your number on WhatsApp without the PIN, but all pending messages received during that time will be deleted.

# 18. Glossary
**Broadcast list:** A list of contacts you can send the same message to at once, without creating a group. Each recipient receives the message as an individual chat.

**Channel:** A one-way broadcast feed where organizations or individuals share updates with followers.

**Community:** A collection of related groups managed under one structure, with a shared announcement group.

**End-to-end encryption:** A security method where only the sender and recipient can read the message content. No one else, including WhatsApp, can access it.

**Group chat:** A conversation shared among multiple participants where everyone can send and receive messages.

**Linked device:** A computer, tablet, or other device connected to your WhatsApp account through WhatsApp Web or the desktop app.

**QR code:** A square barcode you scan with your phone's camera. WhatsApp uses QR codes for linking devices and transferring chats.

**Read receipt:** The blue double ticks that appear when a recipient has read your message.

**Status:** A temporary photo, video, or text post that disappears after 24 hours.

**Two-step verification:** A security feature that adds a six-digit PIN to protect your WhatsApp account from unauthorized registration.

**Voice message:** A recorded audio message you send by holding the microphone button in a chat.

# 20. Getting help
If you run into a problem that this guide does not cover, WhatsApp has a built-in help center:

1. Open Settings > Help > Help Center.

2. Browse the categories or search for your
issue.

3. If you need to contact WhatsApp directly,
go to Settings > Help > Contact Us. Describe your problem, and WhatsApp support will respond via email.

You can also visit the WhatsApp FAQ on the web at faq.whatsapp.com for articles covering setup, security, troubleshooting, and account recovery.
